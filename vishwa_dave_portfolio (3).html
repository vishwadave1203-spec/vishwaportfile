About Me

A writer who lives
between worlds

I am Vishwa Dave — a writer, novelist, and postgraduate literature student at Pandit Deendayal Energy University, studying English Literature with a focus on the intersections of society, identity, and narrative form. I write under the pen name Yukee.

My creative work spans serialised digital fiction — contracted and published on platforms like Webnovel and Hinovel — and my academic work explores Russian, Japanese, and Indian fiction through feminist and philosophical lenses.

I hold a Branch Topper distinction in my Bachelor's degree with a CGPA of 9.14, and I believe the best writing, whether a novel or a dissertation, asks the same question: what does it mean to be human in this particular moment?

01
MA First Dissertation · PDEU, December 2025 · Supervisor: Dr. Annie Rachel Royson
The Spirituality and the Grotesque: Critique of Nineteenth Century Russian Society in Nikolai Gogol's Stories

This dissertation argues that Nikolai Gogol constructs an unmistakable spiritual semiotic system based on the grotesque as the primary means through which he communicates the absence of the divine — and, conversely, expresses his belief in God through negative theology. Examining "The Nose" (1836), "The Overcoat" (1842), "Nevsky Prospekt" (1835), "The Portrait" (1835), and "Viy" (1835), the study demonstrates how deformation, fragmentation, and parody function not as comic devices but as signs of collapsed theology. The dissertation synthesises orthodox apophatic theology, cultural-historical theory, and psychoanalytic analysis to present Gogol as a forerunner of modernist metaphysics — one for whom the grotesque is ultimately the only available language of the sacred.

02
MA Second Dissertation · PDEU, May 2026 · Supervisor: Dr. Nigam Dave
Fragile Masculinity and Self-destructive Subjectivity: A Comparative Study of Devdas and No Longer Human

This dissertation examines fragile masculinity and self-destructive subjectivity in Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay's Devdas and Osamu Dazai's No Longer Human through a comparative literary approach. Although rooted in different socio-cultural contexts — late colonial Bengal and post-war Japan — both novels portray male protagonists who experience alienation, emotional paralysis, shame, and gradual self-destruction. The study analyses alcoholism, passivity, emotional repression, and suicidal tendencies to understand how masculine identity becomes linked with self-destructive behaviour, while also examining the role of female characters in exposing and challenging masculine fragility. Placing these novels within a cross-cultural comparative framework, the dissertation contributes to the study of modern Asian literature.

03
